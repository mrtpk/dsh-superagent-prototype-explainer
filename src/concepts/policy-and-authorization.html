The idea

{{term policy | Policy}} is the versioned rule set the Engine reads, plus each Source's own part of it ({{cite src/core/ports.ts:17-27}}). {{term admission | admission}}, the gate, is its first layer: a deterministic check of who may send a Trigger, and with what {{term strength | strength}} ({{cite src/policy.ts:191-211}}). {{term eval | Eval}} then asks for an assessment of the admissible batch, and ends with one {{term decision | Decision}} ({{cite src/core/evals.ts:21-26}}). Only an accept Decision grants a {{term mandate | Mandate}}: the strength, scope and Policy version the work relies on ({{cite src/core/model.ts:126-133}}).

An everyday comparison. Think of a building's front desk as the gate: it checks identity against a list before anyone goes further. A supervisor, the assessment, then judges the specific request. Only a signed visitor badge, the Mandate, states its own scope and lets the visit continue. The comparison stops at memory: the gate keeps none of its own. It is a pure function of the Trigger, the Thread and the open Tickets on it ({{cite src/core/ports.ts:17-27}}).

In the code

Step 1: the gate judges each queued Trigger

Evals.start() calls policy.gate() once for each pending Trigger on the Thread ({{cite src/core/evals.ts:96}}). It keeps the strongest admissible one, by strength and then by time ({{cite src/core/evals.ts:95-101}}). A human Trigger's gate checks explicit addressing first, then a Thread follow-up, then the admission table, in that fixed order.

A non-human Trigger takes a separate path, machineGate(), which admits only listed bots and senderless events the Source observes, always as inferred ({{cite src/policy.ts:171-188}}).

Step 2: Eval records a Decision for the gate's result

GateResult is either admissible: false with a reason, or admissible: true with a strength and a basis ({{cite src/core/ports.ts:7}}). Evals.decide() applies the assessor's outcome to that Eval and records a Decision ({{cite src/core/evals.ts:131-156}}). It refuses to accept an inferred candidate unless Policy.admitsInferred() allows it ({{cite src/core/evals.ts:151-154}}). That needs the accept mode to be auto, with the matched rules still enabled. assessmentOutcome() itself demotes an inferred accept to an ignored would_accept Decision whenever the accept mode is not auto ({{cite src/policy.ts:55-58}}).

Step 3: an accept decision builds the Mandate

Evals.accept() copies the Eval's strength, actor, Policy version and basis straight into a new Mandate ({{cite src/core/evals.ts:306-316}}). A Mandate also records scope: what work the accept covers ({{cite src/core/model.ts:126-133}}). It never changes after that, whatever happens to the Ticket it bounds ({{cite src/core/model.ts:125}}).

Step 4: strength, and what the admission table decides

A Mandate's {{term strength | strength}} is explicit or inferred; there is no third, weaker value ({{cite src/core/model.ts:96}}). Not admitted at all is GateResult's admissible: false branch, which carries no strength and grants no Mandate ({{cite src/core/ports.ts:7}}). The admission table itself has up to three cells per receiving scope, each mapped to explicit, inferred or ignore ({{cite src/admission.ts:9-13}}). policy.ts's admissionCell() and admissionOf() pick the right cell for a sender and look up its value ({{cite src/policy.ts:135-140}}).

More detail

The accept mode, off, observe or auto, is a Policy-wide switch, default observe ({{cite src/policy.ts:84}}). It decides whether the gate ever admits anything inferred at all ({{cite src/policy.ts:179}}, {{cite src/policy.ts:184}}), and whether an inferred accept is honored rather than merely recorded as would_accept ({{cite src/policy.ts:55-58}}). The Policy version, a hash of the rules, the accept mode and each Source's part, is recorded on every Mandate and Decision ({{cite src/policy.ts:116-118}}). A version change does not revoke a live Mandate by itself; policyEffects() only records what changed, as an Input the Ticket's Executor reads ({{cite src/policy.ts:144-165}}). Eval's own timeout is a separate knob: eval.timeoutSeconds, default 120, becomes evalTimeoutMs once the candidate is delivered to the assessor ({{cite src/policy.ts:85}}, {{cite src/policy.ts:190}}).

What can go wrong

Weakening a rule or an admission cell does not touch an open Ticket's Mandate right away. Only revalidation before a resume, or a person's stop, can end it ({{cite src/policy.ts:213-239}}).
  • An inferred accept is refused outside auto mode, even if the assessment itself says accept. Evals.decide() throws before recording anything, unless Policy.admitsInferred() allows the matched rules ({{cite src/core/evals.ts:151-154}}).
  • Explicit admission always wins over a Thread follow-up, never the reverse. The gate checks the explicit-addressed case first, so a Thread's existing Tickets can never pull an explicit request down to inferred strength ({{cite src/policy.ts:203-204}}).

This page's basis table leaves out via: 'listed', which the code's own comment says is for a Mandate accepted before Mandates recorded their basis ({{cite src/core/model.ts:112-113}}).

Why it matters. Assuming a Policy change acts immediately can mislead a person two ways. They might think a Ticket lost authority it still has. Or they might trust one that a resume would in fact refuse.

What does an accept Decision grant that nothing else does?

A Mandate: the strength, scope and Policy version the work relies on ({{cite src/core/model.ts:126-133}}). Nothing else creates one.

 What are the two Strength values a Mandate can record?

explicit and inferred ({{cite src/core/model.ts:96}}). Not admitted at all is GateResult's admissible: false branch, which carries no strength ({{cite src/core/ports.ts:7}}).

 In what order does the gate check a human Trigger?

Explicit addressing first, then a Thread follow-up, then the admission table ({{cite src/policy.ts:191-211}}). Explicit admission is checked before the follow-up case, so a Thread's own Tickets can never weaken an explicit request to inferred strength ({{cite src/policy.ts:203-204}}).

 Why can switching the accept mode from auto to observe not immediately end an open Ticket's authority?

Revalidation runs only before a resume, not on a live Ticket ({{cite src/policy.ts:213-221}}). A mode change only makes policyEffects() record a proactive: false advisory Input for the Ticket, which its Executor reads; it does not revoke the Mandate ({{cite src/policy.ts:160}}).
